# RASML — Required Advisory Statements for Medicine Labels
## Therapeutic Goods (Medicines Advisory Statements) Specification 2021
## Schedule 1 — RASML No. 6 | Authorised Version F2021L01888 | Registered 23/12/2021

> **How to use this reference:**
> When reviewing a TGO 92 non-prescription medicine label, check the active ingredient(s) against this file. If the substance is listed here, the required statements in Column 4 must appear on the label under the "Warnings" heading of the CHI table (or on the main label as specified). Check whether the circumstances in Column 3 match the product being reviewed.
>
> **Important:** Advisory statements may be varied in wording provided the intent is not changed (s.7(2)(a)). Two or more statements may be combined into a simple sentence provided intent is not changed (s.7(2)(b)).
>
> **Transition period:** RASML 2021 replaced RASML 2019. Transition ended 30 June 2023 — all labels must now carry RASML 2021 statements.

## Symbol Key

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| [c] | Statement must be included at the beginning of the directions for use on the label |
| [f] | Statement must appear immediately below the "KEEP OUT OF REACH OF CHILDREN" cautionary statement; in bold-face sanserif capital letters of uniform thickness; in letters at least 0.4× the height of the signal word letters; with no other statement on the same line |
| [g] | Statement, written in sanserif capital letters, must be included on the main label or as part of the directions for use |

## Full Required Statements — High-Frequency OTC Active Ingredients

### PARACETAMOL

**Item 191 — Unscheduled (for exclusion from schedules), oral use**
- Adults: Keep to the recommended dose. Do not take this medicine for longer than a few days at a time unless advised to by a doctor.
- Children and adolescents: Keep to the recommended dose. Do not give this medicine for longer than 48 hours at a time unless advised to by a doctor.
- If an overdose is taken or suspected, ring the Poisons Information Centre (Australia 13 11 26, New Zealand 0800 764 766) or go to hospital straight away even if you feel well because of the risk of delayed, serious liver damage.
- Do not take with other products containing paracetamol, unless advised to do so by a doctor or pharmacist.

**Item 192 — Schedule 2 or Schedule 3, oral use**
- Same as Item 191 (either or both dosage/overdose statements apply)
- Do not take with other products containing paracetamol, unless advised to do so by a doctor or pharmacist.

**Item 193 — Paracetamol in combination with ibuprofen, oral use**
- Do not give to children under 12 years of age.
- Adults: Keep to the recommended dose. Do not take this medicine for longer than a few days at a time unless advised to by a doctor.
- Children and adolescents: Keep to the recommended dose. Do not give this medicine for longer than 48 hours at a time unless advised to by a doctor.
- Excessive use can be harmful and increase the risk of heart attack, stroke or liver damage.
- Do not use if pregnant or trying to become pregnant.
- Do not use if you have a stomach ulcer / impaired kidney function / heart failure.
- Do not use if you are allergic to ibuprofen or other anti-inflammatory medicines.
- If you get an allergic reaction, stop taking and see your doctor immediately.
- Unless a doctor has told you to, do not use if you have asthma.
- Unless a doctor has told you to, do not use if you are aged 65 years or over.
- Do not take with other products containing paracetamol, ibuprofen, aspirin or other anti-inflammatory medicines or with medicines that you are taking regularly, unless advised to do so by a doctor or pharmacist.
- If an overdose is taken or suspected, ring the Poisons Information Centre (Australia 13 11 26, New Zealand 0800 764 766) or go to hospital straight away even if you feel well because of the risk of delayed, serious liver damage.

### IBUPROFEN

**Item 128 — Unscheduled, oral use, adults and children ≥12 years**
- Do not use if you have a stomach ulcer / impaired kidney function / heart failure.
- Do not use if you are allergic to ibuprofen or other anti-inflammatory medicines.
- If you get an allergic reaction, stop taking and see your doctor immediately.
- Unless a doctor has told you to, do not use if you have asthma.
- Unless advised by your doctor or pharmacist, do not use with products containing ibuprofen, aspirin or other anti-inflammatory medicines or with medicines that you are taking regularly.
- Do not use for more than a few days at a time unless a doctor has told you to. Do not exceed the recommended dose. Excessive use can be harmful and increase the risk of heart attack, stroke or liver damage.
- Do not use if trying to become pregnant, or during the first 6 months of pregnancy, except on doctor's advice. Do not use at all during the last 3 months of pregnancy.

**Items 130–131 — Oral use in children under 12 years**

- Do not use in children 6 years of age or less.

**Item 133 — Dermal (topical) use**

## How to Apply RASML in a Review

1. **Identify active ingredients** from the label or product information.
2. **Check this index** — if the substance appears, locate its specific entry or entries.
3. **Determine which circumstances apply** — many substances have multiple entries based on schedule, age group, indication, or route of administration.
4. **Check the Warnings section of the CHI table** — all applicable RASML statements must appear there.
5. **Check wording** — exact wording is not mandatory (minor variations that preserve intent are permitted), but intent must be preserved.
6. **Note:** If the full statement list cannot be verified from the artwork provided (e.g., CHI panel not visible), flag this as unable to verify and request the full artwork or approved labelling.
